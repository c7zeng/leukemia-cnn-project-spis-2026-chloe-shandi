Lab Writeup

Using convolutional neural networks to classify microscopic blood-cell images.

01

Project Description

The goal of this machine learning project is to build a convolutional neural network (CNN) capable of analyzing microscopic blood-cell images and predicting whether cells display signs of acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL), a common pediatric cancer.

The scans were taken from imaging documentation collected by the Cancer Imaging Archive. The dataset contains ground-truth labels annotated by an oncologist, allowing each image to be associated with its true classification.

Because of morphological similarity and the sheer quantity of blood cells requiring inspection, a machine learning model capable of assisting an oncologist—or serving as an initial screening tool—could potentially improve the efficiency of cancer-image analysis.

02

Process

Building this model required becoming familiar with TensorFlow, NumPy, Matplotlib, Keras, Scikit-learn, and Pillow. These libraries allowed us to manipulate images, organize data into arrays, construct CNN models, train them, and visualize training progress.

Image Preprocessing

The first step was to preprocess all 10,661 raw 300 × 300 pixel color images into a format that could be efficiently processed by the CNN.

We cropped the images to reduce the extra black space surrounding each cell. The images were then resized to 128 × 128 pixels and converted to grayscale. This reduced the amount of data that needed to be stored and processed while preserving important morphological information.

03

Model Implementation

We began with a simple model to test our data-processing pipeline. We then experimented with different layers, layer sizes, kernel sizes, optimization algorithms, and loss calculations to investigate how these choices affected model performance and runtime.

We also implemented stratified 3-fold cross-validation to obtain a more reliable estimate of model performance and reduce the influence of chance variation in the training and validation splits.

04

Results

72.33% Mean Balanced Accuracy

After training, the CNN achieved a mean balanced accuracy of 72.33%. This is above the 50% chance level expected for a binary classification problem.

However, this level of accuracy would not be sufficient for clinical application. A model intended to assist with medical diagnosis would require substantially higher accuracy, extensive validation, and significantly more training data and computational resources.
